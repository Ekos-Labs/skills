# Music and Sound Direction

## Background music

Use a restrained electronic track at roughly 95 to 110 BPM. It should begin
with tension and fragmented digital texture, gain a controlled pulse when Ekos
is introduced, and become wider and more confident as the engine assembles.

The track should feel precise, modern, and slightly industrial. Avoid corporate
uplifting music, festival drops, trap drums, sentimental piano, and an epic
movie-trailer build. The voice must remain the dominant element.

Choose a licensed track with stems or, at minimum, a clean instrumental WAV.
Useful search language for a music library:

- minimal technology pulse
- dark innovation electronic
- restrained industrial future
- cinematic product reveal without vocals
- evolving modular synth 100 BPM

Preferred musical shape:

1. **AI montage:** filtered noise, fragmented pulse, little or no bassline.
2. **“But the biggest companies”:** pulse drops back so the thesis lands.
3. **“That is why I am building Ekos Labs”:** tonal lift and first full bass note.
4. **Engine explanation:** steady rhythmic bed with quiet percussive detail.
5. **Proof:** simplify the music and let each result land clearly.
6. **Channels and products:** widest musical section, still below the voice.
7. **Ekos close:** remove percussion, hold one resolved chord, then end cleanly.

## Sound palette

Build one related family of sounds rather than collecting unrelated effects:

- Dry broadcast cuts for the four opening “AI” clips.
- Short low impacts between those cuts.
- One reverse tonal rise into the Ekos reveal.
- Soft glass or ceramic ticks for cards.
- Muted data clicks for signals and ICP nodes.
- Directional sweeps for connections travelling through the engine.
- A warm confirmation tone when a channel locks into place.
- Two related product chimes for Pocket CMO and Remy.
- A low, clean logo resolve at the end.

Avoid glitch spam, keyboard sounds on every interaction, loud cinematic booms,
and effects that compete with consonants in the dialogue.

## Mix targets

- Dialogue stays centred and consistent throughout.
- Duck music by roughly 3 to 5 dB under dense spoken sections.
- Use sound effects selectively at visual state changes.
- Keep room tone underneath full-screen Remotion sections so the film remains
  connected to the original recording.
- Deliver dialogue, music, effects, and AI montage as separate stems.

Exact cue frames will be set from the final edited performance. Do not lock
sound effects to estimated script timings.
