# Routing rules

Use this table to compare explanations, not as a rigid funnel or a reason to ask for every metric. Pick the earliest constraint with evidence that is both consequential and actionable. A later constraint can dominate when it makes acquisition spend wasteful.

| Evidence pattern | More likely constraint | First useful work | What could overturn it |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Target buyers do not recognise the problem or cannot identify themselves in the offer | Buyer/problem understanding | Interview or review-mining brief; segment map | Qualified buyers already describe the problem clearly |
| People see the offer but cannot explain who it is for or why it beats alternatives | Positioning/message | Compare current copy with buyer language and proof | Strong qualified conversion despite unclear wording |
| Clear offer and proof, but too few relevant people encounter it | Reach/distribution | Test one channel where the actual buyer already looks, asks, or compares | Existing traffic is adequate but leaks later |
| Relevant visitors or leads arrive but very few take the next step | Conversion/friction | Observe the page or sales step, then fix the biggest objection or broken path | Traffic is unqualified or the sample is too small |
| Leads show interest but stall in follow-up, pilots, carts, or procurement | Sales/activation | Map handoff, response time, objections, and one next-step experiment | Customers who get through still fail to retain value |
| People buy or try but do not return, use, or renew | Product/retention | Inspect fulfilment and value delivery before increasing acquisition | A known billing or tracking issue explains the apparent drop |

## Channel-fit checks

Choose the channel from buyer behavior, product economics, and geography, not from what the agent can automate.

- B2B, narrow and high-value: direct introductions, relevant communities, targeted outbound, partner channels, and search where demand exists can all fit. LinkedIn is useful only when the actual decision maker is active there.
- B2C or consumer apps: creator content, search, app stores, communities, referrals, and paid acquisition may fit. A Product Hunt launch is a one-off event, not a default acquisition engine.
- Physical goods: distinguish marketplace demand, search/shopping intent, retail distribution, creator demonstrations, local discovery, and repeat purchase. Do not substitute software-style founder outbound by default.
- Local services or emerging markets: Google Maps/local search, WhatsApp or calls, referrals, and trusted local partners may beat email or LinkedIn. Confirm local norms, consent rules, and access before recommending a channel.
- If channel economics, customer location, or purchasing role is unknown, recommend a small evidence-gathering test, not a scaled campaign.

The router's job is to tell the user **which work to do next and why**. It does not claim a channel is profitable, promise virality, or run execution without the user's approval.
