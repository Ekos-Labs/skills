# Attention principles

The reasoning under every line of a script. Apply it silently. It is not a template, and it never replaces the workflow's structure:

1. **Hook:** an immediately clear, valuable gap in what the viewer knows.
2. **Conflict:** the tension or contradiction that makes them need the answer.
3. **Pattern:** the mechanism, lesson, sequence or lived proof.
4. **Bridge back:** the viewer's next action, or the next open question.

Use the principles below inside each of these beats. Do not swap the structure for a generic hook, value, CTA formula.

## The viewer

- Write to one person, as if the reel is being sent straight to them. Never to "an audience".
- Specifics belong in the object: the proof, result, date, number, place, action or screenshot. Do not spend specificity on audience labels.
- The viewer asks two questions the whole time: is this valuable, and how much time will it cost me? Make the value feel large and the time feel contained.
- The hook must put a real question in their head. Announcing a topic is not a hook.

## Line by line

- A reel is taken in one line at a time. Every line must make the next one necessary.
- Open a new curiosity loop before the previous one fully closes.
- Pay every loop honestly. Never set up a real promise and deflate it with a joke or a vague reveal.
- Give a concrete insight, proof or surprise in the first 5 to 10 seconds. Do not make them wait for the value.
- If a 10-year-old could not understand the sentence on its own, make the sentence clearer without making the idea smaller.

## Writing

- Replace broad summaries with things people can picture: the exact outcome, time, number, name, result, place or action.
- Cut any line that does not serve the hook's central promise, even if it is personally interesting.
- Find the most likely reason the viewer would scroll ("this is an ad", "this is too complicated", "this only works because he has money or a team") and disarm it where it comes up.
- Write short, clear spoken lines. Keep the creator's own words, specifics, experience and humour. Add only the connecting words needed to say it out loud.
- Give the script distinct beats, so the edit has natural places to change the frame. Cuts refresh attention, but they cannot save a script with no open loop.

## Review before showing a script

Ask yourself, silently:
1. What exact question is the viewer holding after the hook?
2. Do the first 10 seconds already prove or teach something concrete?
3. Does each line sell the next one?
4. Where does the next loop open before the current one resolves?
5. Which line can be more concrete, or cut?
6. What objection does the reel create, and where is it answered?

These are strong defaults, not laws. A great reel can break them. Never optimise for the checklist over the creator's real story and the real viewer.
