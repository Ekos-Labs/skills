# Outlier reel patterns

Read from 48 transcripts of short-form reels that performed far above their account's size: 250K+ views, and in about two-thirds of them 2x or more the account's followers. Most are founders, students and operators talking to camera about careers, building and money. 33 of the 48 are personal talking-head or explainer reels; the rest are skits, interview clips or unusable. The patterns below come from those 33. Skits and clips follow different rules.

Use these as the shapes to model. For each new script, still find and name a current outlier in the creator's own niche (see the workflow in SKILL.md). These patterns tell you what to look for.

## Hook (0 to 3 s): the first line carries a contradiction or a gap

Held in 31 of 33 reels.
- **Result next to an unexpected state:** a big result while doing something ordinary ("a big name replied while I sat on the couch").
- **Age plus status:** the strongest opener in the set ("I'm 22 and I run a ...", "20-year-old finance student"). It makes the viewer a peer in three seconds.
- **Shock statement plus identity stack:** "I'm going to drop out. I'm 21, a student, and I work at a top company."
- **A question people ask, or a scenario question:** "everyone asks me X", "I just landed with nothing, where would you start?"
- **Contrarian or truth-telling opener:** "is it just me or has X become over-glamorised", "it's not what you think".
- **Named audience plus guide title plus enemy list:** "[audience] edition, no A, no B, just the real version".
- **Offer first:** a specific offer with every barrier removed, then proof, then steps, then CTA.
- **Second-person relatable list:** "you work all day, your calendar looks like a puzzle..." ending on "if that's you, I'm [name]".
- **Reaction or quote:** "someone online said X, I sat there for a second".
- **Emotional repetition:** the same sentence start three or four times, no tactics.

## Bridge (3 to 10 s): one or two sentences, about 5 to 10% of the runtime

Turns the hook's tension into a promise, gives a reason to stay, and names what is coming. Present in nearly every reel over about 45 s; reels of 17 to 39 s often skip it.
- **Time or cost anchor:** "four months ago...", "it took a lot of sacrifice".
- **Reframe promise:** "this will change how you think about X, it's not A, it's B".
- **Belief reversal:** "most people do X, that's the wrong approach".
- **Stakes:** "this matters, because if X, no one will take you seriously".
- **Answer tease:** "the answer is simple".
- **Personal position:** "I don't think I have to pick one".
- **Polarity:** "listen or don't, I don't care".

## Conflict: one named obstacle, never several

- A false belief to overturn (most common).
- An outsider who can't get in, solved by a method.
- An either/or pressure (school or career, workload).
- The viewer's own pain ("why you feel invisible").
- An origin gap (first in the family, fear of posting).
- A broken system (the degree gate).
- Cost or sacrifice (family, comfort).
- A hollow win, a valley (kicked out, embarrassed), or "success looks effortless".

## Story shapes

- **Proof mechanism:** claim, obstacle, method in steps, proof on screen, so-what. For demos and tutorials.
- **Reframe:** question, reversal, analogy or list of evidence, a one-line instruction. Short, 25 to 60 s.
- **Ranked list:** items from easiest to hardest, each with requirement, benefit and catch, then "save this".
- **Lifestyle proof:** proof, then cause, then origin, then an action for the viewer.
- **Age-stamped timeline:** milestones by age, with one valley and one turn. The age stamps are the open loops.
- **Icon comparison, then equaliser:** "this person also started from zero, just like you", then "now go work at it".
- **One-liner reversal (about 17 s):** a quoted belief, then the flip.
- **News or discovery explainer:** "X just revealed...", what it is, a one-line analogy, why it matters, the tie to the viewer.
- **Pure emotion:** a repeated line and a purpose, no tactics (25 to 55 s).

## Retention

- Open loops: numbered steps, "next up", a withheld secret or a one-word teaser. A new loop about every 10 seconds.
- Proof shown on screen around the midpoint (messages, replies, dashboards), not just told.
- Specific numbers in nearly every reel.
- The payoff by about two-thirds of the way, then a short closing line.
- A visual change every few seconds: cutaways, icons, caption words.

## Polarity targets seen

What the reels push against: the default path (degree, job, "worst case you get a job"), tool obsession ("it's the person, not the stack"), common advice ("it's not niches or hooks, it's one viewer"), AI slop, broken systems.

## Closes seen

A keyword CTA, a free-guide CTA, "save this", "follow, the story unfolds", an instruction ("now go and work at it"), a punchline, or a closing meaning with no CTA.

## Length

Median about 60 s; half at 60 s or less; range 17 to 103 s. Story reels cluster at 55 to 70 s; demos and day-logs run 90 to 140 s. The workflow defaults to 40 to 50 s because shorter is easier to finish watching. Go longer only when the story shape needs it.

## Pattern check for every hook, bridge and story

1. The first line has a contradiction or a gap, ideally age plus status.
2. Over about 45 s: a one or two sentence bridge (promise, reversal, stakes or cost).
3. One named conflict.
4. Resolution by proof, numbers or a ranked list, with a new loop about every 10 seconds.
5. A close: keyword, instruction, follow, or a closing meaning.
6. One polarity target, picked on purpose.
